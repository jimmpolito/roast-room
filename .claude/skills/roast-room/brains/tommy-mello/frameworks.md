# Frameworks: Tommy Mello

Built from the four batches of notes on S001 to S016 (his own Q&A podcast episodes 2021 to 2026, plus interviews). 20 frameworks, roughly most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea (ids listed). He rarely names his tools, so most names are short descriptive ones marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes. His numbers are his own claims about A1 Garage Door Service, not audited figures.

---

## 1. The four KPIs (the funnel scoreboard)
- **What it is:** Every home service business runs on four numbers: booking rate, conversion rate, average ticket, cost per acquisition. Nail them, then pour fuel on. He reads every growth, pricing, and marketing question through these first.
- **Parts:** Booking rate (any call or form that books a visit to the home); conversion rate at the kitchen table, split into service and equipment or door sales (he counts anything over $80 on a service call as a conversion, S011); average ticket; cost per acquisition. Service-to-sales (fix or replace) is the gross close rate.
- **When to use:** Any plan to grow, buy leads, raise prices, open a market, or buy a company. Find the weakest of the four and put everything there.
- **How to score:** Guidance from his benchmarks. Booking rate 75% good, 85% great, about 90% target; above 90% may mean booking calls not worth it (S004, S014); A1 claims 91% (S016). Service conversion above 90% to 95% (S009, S014). Equipment or door conversion about 60% to 70% (S009, S014). Average ticket over $1,000 before paid ads make sense (S009). A plan that adds leads while any of these sits below benchmark scores low.
- **Quote:** "The four KPIs I care about. Average ticket, conversion rate, booking rate, and cost per acquisition." (S004)
- **Sources:** 10 (S004, S006, S008, S009, S010, S011, S014, S015, S016, S012)

## 2. Call center first (booking-rate math) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The phones are the cheapest place to find money. Each point of booking rate is worth 1% of revenue, and a lost call already paid for in marketing is pure waste.
- **Parts:** Measure abandonment and booking rate; compare what an unanswered call costs (lead cost times ticket times close rate) with the pay for staffing the phones; pay CSRs per booked call or a share of the extra profit (about 10%); fix speed to lead and after-hours coverage; add AI and call scoring.
- **When to use:** Any business he looks at, any acquisition, and before any increase in lead spend.
- **How to score:** On a $10M company each booking point is $100,000 (S006). If the cost of two extra CSR hours is less than the calls they would book, staff them (S014). Abandonment above a few percent is a red flag (he cut his from 10% to 1.5%, S004).
- **Quote:** "booking rates are where I see the biggest opportunity for every business I look at." (S014)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S006, S009, S010, S013, S014, S016, S005)

## 3. Back into price from gross margin (price book) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Price is set by the margin you need to fund the whole business (trainers, recruiters, trucks, training center, marketing, CRM), not by competitors. Pay plans then follow from the price book.
- **Parts:** Burden labor cost, acquisition cost, overhead as a share of ticket; target gross margin; build the price book; derive tech pay from it.
- **When to use:** Owner is barely profitable, wants to match a competitor, or cannot afford to pay people well.
- **How to score:** Guidance: solo operator 65% to 70% gross margin (S003); a company in the low 60s or high 50s gross profit (S010); labor about 28% to 32% of revenue (S013); net under 10% means mispriced (S012). Fix booking and conversion before raising price (S010).
- **Quote:** "You need to back up into your gross margin, and that's how you should do your pricing." (S003)
- **Sources:** 7 (S003, S007, S008, S010, S012, S013, S005)

## 4. Sell oranges, not apples (pick two of three)
- **What it is:** You can be cheapest, fastest, or best quality, never all three. He picks quality and speed and makes the product hard to compare: trademarked or white-labeled parts, longer-cycle springs, better warranty, faster install, bundled extras instead of discounts.
- **Parts:** Choose two; differentiate the product; stock the most-sold items to install in 24 to 72 hours; when pushed on price, hold it and add low-cost, high-perceived-value extras; keep a builder-grade option available.
- **When to use:** Price objections, a cheaper competitor, discount requests from the field.
- **How to score:** High if the customer cannot line up the quote against a rival's like for like; low if the offer is the same part as everyone else at a lower price.
- **Quote:** "I wanted to sell oranges when everybody's selling apples." (S015)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S011, S014, S015, S016, S006)

## 5. Options, not ultimatums (packages from the best down)
- **What it is:** Never present one price. Design the best package first, strip features down to a like-for-like option priced near competitors, and recommend what you would do for your mother.
- **Parts:** Ask questions first (how long in the home, who uses it); three or more options; the four-of-five-star package carries the profit; avoid the word upsell.
- **When to use:** Any sales process, low conversion, discount pressure.
- **How to score:** No options at the kitchen table scores low regardless of price.
- **Quote:** "If you're not giving options, you're giving ultimatums." (S004)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S007, S009, S010, S013, S014)

## 6. Scorecards and performance pay
- **What it is:** Each role gets four to six measurable things it controls, graded against the mean, and pay rises only when performance does. He hates the word commission.
- **Parts:** Techs: average ticket (about 40% weight), conversion, service-to-sales, callbacks, five-star reviews, memberships. CSRs: booking rate, tonality, data accuracy. Pay a percentage of ticket less parts and tax, tiered by level; or sold hours instead of hours worked. Start with an OKR, model it in Excel on history, run old and new pay side by side with a small group, change pay rarely. Scorecards feed one-on-ones that try to change one thing (will or skill).
- **When to use:** Designing comp, low ticket or conversion, an hourly team that is slow.
- **How to score:** Guidance: red flag when people earn more under the hourly floor than under performance pay (S010); if the tech's share is too high, lower the percent and raise price together (S008). Never pay extra for core work like reviews (S007).
- **Quote:** "Every role, there should be four or five, maybe six things, tops." (S008)
- **Sources:** 9 (S003, S004, S007, S008, S009, S010, S013, S014, S015)

## 7. Equity incentive program (profit units, phantom equity)
- **What it is:** Key leaders share only in value created above today's line, paid at a sale or on a vesting schedule, with a stated target. He calls it golden handcuffs.
- **Parts:** Value the company on a multiple; grant nothing below it; give a percentage of growth above; vest over about five years or on EBITDA targets; revalue regularly; use a specialist law firm; honor the payout.
- **When to use:** Retaining and aligning leaders, preparing for private equity, delegating a big project (ask: can they earn equity?).
- **How to score:** No target or no known finish line scores low. His claimed result: EBITDA from about $12M to $27.5M in 19 months after issuing it (S015), and 24 or 25 equity holders paid at the 2022 sale (S015, S016).
- **Quote:** "Your business is worth 10 million today. So you put a line at 10 million, that's yours." (S008)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S004, S006, S008, S009, S010, S014, S015)

## 8. Marketing spend by mode *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Marketing is a set percent of revenue that moves with the goal: hold, grow, or take a market.
- **Parts:** Maintain about 5%; steady 10% (A1 says 12%); grow 20%; take a new market 25% to 35% of the target and accept little profit for a year to a year and a half while the brand builds.
- **When to use:** Budgets, a new market, deciding how hard to push.
- **How to score:** His numbers: "10% minimum, 20% if I'm growing" (S010); new market at least 25% of target (S006), 35% in new A1 markets (S013); mailers under 10% (S004). Below 10% with growth goals scores low. Never finance marketing (S008).
- **Quote:** "10% minimum, 20% if I'm growing, 5% if I'm just dialed in and just trying to maintain." (S010)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S006, S007, S009, S010, S012, S013, S015)

## 9. Follow the lead through the whole cycle (attribution)
- **What it is:** Judge each lead source by the revenue it produces after booking, cancellations, conversion, and ticket, not by cost per lead. Whoever can pay most per lead wins.
- **Parts:** A tracking number per source (A1 claims over 7,000), UTM parameters, revenue-level reporting from agencies, small tests to learn cost per acquisition, demand-driven calls over low-ticket calls.
- **When to use:** Any ad spend, agency review, new channel.
- **How to score:** A source you cannot tie to revenue scores low. Guidance from S012: allowable acquisition cost comes from ticket times close rate. A dollar in for $1.25 out is winning (S006).
- **Quote:** "I don't mind what the cost per lead is. It could be $10,000 if I get $50,000, who cares." (S016)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S004, S006, S009, S012, S013, S014, S016)

## 10. Own the demand, one channel at a time (channel ladder) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Build reputation and Google first, then add channels one at a time; mastered channels compound.
- **Parts:** Reviews everywhere, then Google Business Profile, local service ads, pay-per-click (mainly to fill capacity), organic; brand basics (name, logo, wraps, site) before TV, radio, billboards; social only after Google is won; match ad hours to staffed capacity.
- **When to use:** Small or new companies choosing channels; anyone spraying across social.
- **How to score:** Low if several channels run at once with none mastered, or paid ads run at hours nobody can take the job.
- **Quote:** "you should only go after one marketing source at a time and master it." (S006)
- **Sources:** 8 (S005, S006, S008, S009, S011, S013, S016, S004)

## 11. Own the core market before expanding (market entry test) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Take as much share as possible at home; open a new market only if it can make big profit and you have cash and leaders for it.
- **Parts:** Market must reach about $3M profit and roughly 200,000+ population; competitive and media analysis; four months of payroll plus a cushion; within two hours of home; a branch manager who will move.
- **When to use:** Any expansion, second location, or new trade.
- **How to score:** Guidance: home market not 10 out of 10 scores low; no cash runway scores low.
- **Changed view:** Earlier he expanded fast and closed four markets (about 2018) after an outsider read his P&L (S002, 2024; S015, 2026). Newer stance (S006, 2025-01-24): "I just not a big fan of fast expansion, not like I used to be" (S006).
- **Quote:** "Our rule of thumb now is if you can't get to $3 million of profit in a market" (S009)
- **Sources:** 9 (S002, S006, S007, S009, S010, S012, S014, S015, S008)

## 12. Bulking or getting shredded (profit floor)
- **What it is:** Each year choose to grow revenue fast or grow profit fast, rarely both, and never drop below a profit floor.
- **Parts:** Growth years target about 15%; cutting years 20% to 30%; get shredded the year before a sale; under 10% net, fix profit before growth spending.
- **When to use:** Annual planning, sale prep, deciding whether to borrow or expand.
- **How to score:** Floor is 10% (S006, S007, S012) or 12% (S004). Inconsistency to note: S009 says breaking even while taking share is fine until $200,000 to $300,000 a month.
- **Quote:** "i'm always trying to do grow revenue fast Or grow profit fast Very rarely am I trying to do both" (S006)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S006, S007, S009, S010, S012)

## 13. Greenfield versus acquire and fix
- **What it is:** Buying a company is fast but expensive; he buys only to fix (booking, marketing, trucks, training, one system), and with a proven playbook prefers greenfield.
- **Parts:** Buy box: residential service, lots of leads and short on people, commercial or new construction under 20%, 12 to 18 month payback. Have a 20% bottom-line model and 50+ employees first. Asset purchases; keep the seller six months; unify on one system; about 5x EBITDA debt.
- **When to use:** Any acquisition or roll-up idea.
- **How to score:** His math: $1M of EBITDA costs about $6M to buy versus $1.5M to greenfield (S006).
- **Changed view:** 2023 to 2024 he leaned to buying: "I don't think I'll ever start another business from scratch." (S009, 2024-05-24). Newer (2025-01-24): "There's nothing better than Greenfield." (S006).
- **Quote:** "That's 1.5 million versus 6 million for the acquisition." (S006)
- **Sources:** 9 (S003, S004, S006, S007, S008, S009, S010, S012, S015)

## 14. Capacity planning by jobs per tech *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Ticket drops when techs run too many jobs; hire ahead of the ratio because hiring takes months.
- **Parts:** Track jobs per tech per day; start hiring at 3.7; at over 4, hire two; dispatch owns the route; do not push overloaded techs to book more.
- **When to use:** Booking capacity, "time slot not available", hiring plans.
- **How to score:** About three jobs a day is healthy; six cuts sales in half, nine to a third (S011).
- **Quote:** "If one of my guys are running more than four jobs a day, like let's say it's 4.1, I need to hire two technicians." (S007)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S005, S007, S011, S016)

## 15. Systems with checks (the checker of the checker)
- **What it is:** Chaos comes from no system, the wrong system, or a system nobody follows. Every system needs an independent check.
- **Parts:** Manuals and SOPs (from Al Levi), checklists, KPIs built into the CRM, data integrity checks (geotagged live photos), layered audits (CFO, auditor, bankers, third-party inventory counts), two vendors for everything.
- **When to use:** Diagnosing office chaos, rolling out tools, scaling past the founder.
- **How to score:** Low if a process exists only in someone's head or nobody can tell whether it was followed.
- **Quote:** "There is no system. It's the wrong system or the system is not being followed." (S010)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S006, S007, S010, S014, S015)

## 16. Test in one market, then scale
- **What it is:** Borrowed from private equity: trial a new idea in one market, prove it, then widen.
- **Parts:** One market for three months, then three markets, then company-wide; run many tests and keep the winners.
- **When to use:** New campaigns, pay plans, software, offers.
- **How to score:** A company-wide rollout with no pilot scores low.
- **Quote:** "try it in one market, prove it out for three months, and then we'll try it in three before you go company wide." (S015)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S007, S013, S014, S015)

## 17. Topgrading: A players and the one-to-ten org chart
- **What it is:** Rate every person one to ten against KPIs; anyone under nine gets topgraded. One A player is worth five B players.
- **Parts:** Print the org chart, rate privately with leadership, rate yourself; fire the two or three you know you must; no performance improvement plans; merit over tenure.
- **When to use:** Annual planning, downturns, a stuck team.
- **How to score:** Guidance: anyone under nine is a problem (S012).
- **Quote:** "I used to think 1A equals 3b now I think 1A equals 5 B players" (S002)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S007, S008, S010, S012, S014)

## 18. Recruit like marketing, hire attitude, train the trade
- **What it is:** Market to people who already have jobs, hire for attitude and hospitality, and build technicians in a training center.
- **Parts:** Social media over job boards; relentless follow-up; trial month then training at headquarters; apprentice to lead tech ladder; founder brand for recruiting.
- **When to use:** Labor shortage, hiring experienced techs, high turnover.
- **Quote:** "we go after somebody from hospitality and teach them how to be a mechanic." (S015)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S004, S005, S008, S014, S015)

## 19. Time and delegation tests (hourly rate, CEO's three jobs) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Put an hourly rate on everything you do; keep only what nobody else can do; the CEO's jobs are people, culture, and lead flow.
- **Parts:** Weekly paper review of the calendar; EA first; steps of delegation (what, why, resources, due date, check-ins, reward, sign-off); success guides recorded by video or voice note.
- **When to use:** Owner still in the truck or doing $20 an hour work.
- **How to score:** Guidance: under about $100 an hour, delegate it (S013).
- **Quote:** "See if the hourly rate is not, you know, a hundred bucks or more, why are you doing it?" (S013)
- **Sources:** 7 (S006, S007, S008, S010, S013, S014, S016)

## 20. Reverse-engineer the goal (three ways to make money)
- **What it is:** Work back from the target to calls, leads, budget, hires, and trucks. Only three levers make money: more customers, higher prices, more frequency.
- **Parts:** Managers' budget plus a bigger "Tommy goal" that pays double bonus; always name the bottom-line percent; start with existing customers and memberships before buying leads.
- **When to use:** Annual budgets, a stretch target, choosing the first lever.
- **Quote:** "there's only three ways to make money. Get new clients, charge your clients more, or keep them coming back more frequently." (S009)
- **Sources:** 7 (S003, S004, S006, S009, S011, S013, S014)
