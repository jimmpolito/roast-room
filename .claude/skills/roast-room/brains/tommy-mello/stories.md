# Stories: Tommy Mello

Built from the batch notes on all 16 sources (S001 to S016). 18 stories and cases he reuses, most-used first, each with the lesson he draws and source ids. Figures are his own claims and some differ by source and date; the last section lists them side by side.

1. **Closing four markets.** With a bridge loan, a new building, and markets losing money, a friend who ran a much bigger company spread out the balance sheets and told him: "he goes, you got to close these four markets today. Offer a relocation package." (S002) In S015 the markets are Atlanta, Houston, Dallas, and Tampa, around 2018, with $7,500 relocation per tech and the warning "you're three months from going out of business." (S015) After closing, margin "shot up to 18% to the bottom line" (S002). Lesson: facts beat belief in a turnaround, and opening faster than you can staff and check leaks money: "I tried to do everything in a year that took me five years in these other markets." (S015) Sources: S002, S007, S015. Behind his newer caution on expansion (S006).

2. **Mom on the phones.** After the 2010 partner buyout his mom moved to Arizona and booked calls; customers hugged her when he arrived. Lesson: how the call is booked sets the sale, and booking rate is the first lever. Sources: S005, S009, S011, S013, S015.

3. **Al Levy tears up the business.** Met through the podcast, Al shredded his manuals, told him to stop talking and build systems, and charged $150,000 that Tommy paid from a home equity line at about $17M revenue with little profit (S015). Lesson: write down the book of knowledge in your head; manuals and SOPs come before scale. "99% of the people that get his manuals, they don't do it because they're too lazy to go in and work on the business." (S004) Sources: S004, S006, S007, S015.

4. **Equity incentives and the 2022 sale.** He issued phantom equity at about $12M EBITDA: "within nineteen months of dishing all these out" and "we ran in nineteen months to 27 and a half million." (S015) In 2022 he sold half at a $570M value and rolled the rest (S015). Lesson: a stake in the outcome makes everyone run the same direction, and the real money comes at exit. Sources: S008, S009, S015, S016.

5. **The sticker photo.** Techs uploaded the same photo to fake job documentation, so in 2017 he had uploads blocked and required live, geotagged photos: "And I realized that they were just uploading the same pictures because they're lazy." (S006) Lesson: people game any system that has no check, so build the checker of the checker. Sources: S006, S015.

6. **The solar gate keypad.** He took a gate job outside his trade, got about 20 warranty calls, and lost money (about $20,000 in S007). Lesson: "just because you could doesn't mean you should" (S014). Sources: S007, S014.

7. **Phone abandonment from 10% to 1.5%.** Once the dashboard showed a tenth of demand calls going unanswered, the team fixed it. Lesson: "The first thing people need is systems to see the score." (S004) Sources: S004.

8. **Partner buyout and near failure.** In 2010 his partner offered the business or the debt; he took the business, then lived through a year of not knowing how to pay taxes: "there were so many days that I didn't know how I'm gonna pay my tax bill." (S015) Earlier he wrote payroll checks "from my home equity line to make payroll" (S002). Lesson: growing faster than your hiring and your grasp of the financials nearly kills the company. Sources: S002, S015.

9. **The $100M shop tours (rip off and duplicate).** He cold-called successful owners, bought breakfast, and from 2017 toured every $100M HVAC shop with an empty notebook: "I called r and d, rip off and duplicate." (S015) Lesson: success leaves clues; copy proven systems from people who have done it. Sources: S004, S009, S015.

10. **CSR performance pay rollout.** Moving the call center to performance pay raised CSR pay about 10% within a month, booking rate jumped, and three people quit on day one (S010). Lesson: pay tied to booking works, and the people who leave were the wrong fit. Sources: S010; related $25 per booked after-hours call in S014.

11. **Portland garage door company.** After performance pay and replacing people who would not sell, monthly profit went from $20,000 to $105,000 (S008). Lesson: wrong people block profit more than leads do. Sources: S008.

12. **Thumbtack and the home warranty channel.** He told Thumbtack, "I'll spend 40 grand a month for you right now, today. Show me your leads work." (S005) and they did not convert. He also dropped home warranty work after an analyst showed the opportunity cost: "He showed me the evidence, and I got out of it." (S005) Lesson: judge a lead source by conversion and opportunity cost, not by volume. Sources: S005.

13. **Chirp and abandoned estimates.** A text and video campaign to unclosed estimates recovered about $700K once they found carriers were blocking a third of messages (S013). Lesson: simple follow-up automation on existing leads can add seven figures before you buy any new ones. Sources: S013.

14. **Rebrand.** Al called his brand garbage; he paid KickCharge about $35,000 at about $30M revenue and matched trucks, shirts, and yard signs: "every yard sign matched the truck." (S015) He still calls the A1 name a 2006 mistake he could not undo (S008). Lesson: brand lowers ad cost and lifts ticket. Sources: S008, S015.

15. **Christmas lights and other side lines.** A China sourcing trip and $200K of the wrong lights, exited over OSHA and insurance risk: "But that was just a distraction." (S015) At about 22 he had nine income streams and was poor (S009). Lesson: one thing first, diversify after. Sources: S009, S015.

16. **The Discount Tire recruit.** He approached a worker juggling three cars, pitched a six-figure path, and followed up daily with a question mark and a video of a top tech (S002). Lesson: recruit people who already work hard, and follow up relentlessly. Related: hire from hospitality and teach the trade (S015). Sources: S002, S015.

17. **Leaders eat last.** He lived in the techs' training apartments for four years and drove a high-mileage truck: "And I wouldn’t buy a new truck till every technician and installer had a new truck" (S002). A friend reminded him he stayed in the old office long after he could move (S006). Lesson: put cash into the team and the fleet before yourself. Sources: S002, S006.

18. **Firehouse springs and 11-gauge hinges.** He pitched red powder-coated, 100,000-cycle spring packages to firehouses and asked a manufacturer for hinges no one else had (S003). The same move shows up as 80,000-cycle springs against a competitor's 10,000-cycle quote (S011, S015). Lesson: sell oranges so no one can compare your price. Sources: S003, S011, S015.

- **Company size over time:** about $5M in 2010 to 2011 (S011, 2021-10); $151M budget for the year (S003, 2022-01); "$200 million company in 22 states" per his book plug (S013, 2025-02); described by the host as $220M (S002, 2024-11, intro, not his words); "300,000,000" as his current base (S015, 2026-02).
- **EBITDA:** a little over $12M to $27M after the right CFO and the equity program (S009, 2024-05); $12M to $27.5M in nineteen months (S015, 2026-02).
- **Branding spend:** $7,000 credited for A1's scale (S008, 2023-08) against about $35,000 to KickCharge at $30M revenue (S015, 2026-02). These may be two different projects.
- **Call volume:** "26,000 calls a month and book 20,000 new clients per month" (S002, 2024-11); 91% booking rate (S016, 2026-10).
