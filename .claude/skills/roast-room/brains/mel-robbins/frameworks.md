# Frameworks: Mel Robbins

Built from the four notes batches on 16 podcast episodes (S001 to S016, 2022-10 to 2026-06). 22 frameworks: her own tools first, roughly most-used first, then guest frameworks she clearly adopts, each labeled as the guest's. "Sources" counts distinct episodes where the idea is used (ids listed). Names are hers where she named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from her stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. The 5 Second Rule
- **What it is:** When you feel an instinct to act on something you know you should do, count backwards 5-4-3-2-1 and physically move on 1. The count interrupts the hesitation window before the brain starts arguing, and counting is itself an action. Her first book is built on it.
- **Parts:** Notice the instinct to act; count 5-4-3-2-1; move on 1, before you feel like it; repeat it as a rep until acting first becomes the habit. Behind it sit her ideas of the five second moment of hesitation (bias toward thinking versus bias toward action), activation energy, and the count as a starting ritual that breaks an old pattern.
- **When to use:** Any moment where the founder knows the move and is stalling: the cold call, the hard conversation with a partner or investor, the layoff, shipping, speaking up. She says people use it to sell, make cold calls and reach executives (S001, paraphrase), and that her husband used it to renegotiate leases and face investors (S001).
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder can name the move and will take it today; 3 if the move is clear but scheduled for "after more research"; 1 if they are still waiting to feel ready. Her threshold is five seconds: past that, the brain talks you out of it.
- **Quote:** "The moment you have an instinct to act, you got to move within five seconds or else your brain will kill it." (S001)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S003, S006, S011). S003 ties it to the "just" in Just Do It; S006 closes with the countdown; S011 retells its origin in her $800K debt year.

## 2. Friction list *(two columns; she also runs it as a yearly audit)*
- **What it is:** Draw a line down a page. Left column: everything that creates friction, resentment or annoyance (work, money, body, relationships). Right column: what you love. Every item on the left becomes a project. A mentor, Peter Sheehan, had her do it in 2019 (S006, S014).
- **Parts:** Write the friction down; for each item find the cause (a broken process, something no longer aligned with you, or the wrong people); then remove it, hire it out, or accept it; repeat every year.
- **When to use:** The founder feels stuck, resentful or vaguely miserable while things look fine on paper; before setting goals for a new year.
- **How to score:** High if each frustration has a named cause and an owner; low if the founder describes a general mood with no list.
- **Quote:** "Wherever you have friction, there is either a broken process, or there is something that is no longer aligned with you, or there are the wrong people around you." (S014)
- **Sources:** 3 (S006, S014, S016). In S006 the three-step version (find, intervene, confirm) is guest Adam Alter's; Mel says "I do it every year." (S006)

## 3. Project, process, people *(the three P's; four P's for a business)*
- **What it is:** Her diagnostic for frustration or a slow business. Turn it into a project instead of a verdict on you, then check the process, then the people. For a business slump she adds product.
- **Parts:** Project (treat it as research, not personal); process (systems, funnel, shipping); product (is it stale, business only); people (too many, the wrong ones, or you).
- **When to use:** Revenue stalls, a team outgrows its systems, the founder blames themselves. She uses it to decide whether to push through, pivot or close (S003).
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has checked all four and found the broken one; 3 if they checked one; 1 if they have concluded they are the problem without checking the system.
- **Quote:** "So I want you to use the four P's, project, process, product, and people." (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S014, S016)

## 4. Small daily action *(unnamed: 15 minutes a day, hot 15)*
- **What it is:** Put a small fixed dose of time on the big thing every day, even if it is only research. She spent about 15 minutes a day for a year before launching her podcast.
- **Parts:** Name the thing you want; give it 5 to 15 minutes every day before email; treat it as an experiment, not a test; keep going for months.
- **When to use:** A founder carrying an idea for years, or overwhelmed by a big goal with no start date.
- **How to score:** Guidance from her numbers: at least 15 minutes a day on the thing scores high; "when things calm down" scores low.
- **Quote:** "I would wake up every day and I would take 15 minutes of action a day." (S006)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S006, S011)

## 5. The Let Them Theory (Let Them, Let Me)
- **What it is:** You cannot control other people, so let them think, feel and do what they will, and use "let me" for your own choices. Her third book.
- **Parts:** Four things you are not responsible for: other people's happiness, rescuing people from their problems, making people understand your choices, proving your worth. When you hear yourself say "I am responsible for", check whether it was ever yours.
- **When to use:** The founder is stalled by what family, peers or old colleagues will think; is underpricing for friends; is carrying a cofounder or employee who will not do the work.
- **How to score:** High if the decision would stand even if everyone around them disapproved; low if the main input is avoiding someone's disappointment.
- **Quote:** "I think you've confused caring about people with carrying their burden." (S009)
- **Sources:** 1 teaching it (S009); the book is promoted in S010.

## 6. Excuses are fear / action is the answer *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Every excuse (too late, too early, someone already did it, I don't know how) is fear being managed. Fear fades once you act, so the answer to an excuse is a step, not a better reason.
- **Parts:** Name the excuse; name the fear under it (failing, being late, upsetting people); reverse engineer the step by studying someone who did it; take one step.
- **When to use:** A founder lists reasons the timing or market is wrong for a thing they clearly want.
- **How to score:** If the objection would vanish after a small action (a call, a test, a post), it is an excuse and scores as fear, not analysis.
- **Quote:** "Any excuse that you have is just fear." (S014)
- **Sources:** 4 (S008, S010, S012, S014)

## 7. The bridge *(how confidence is built)*
- **What it is:** Confidence is the result, not the entry ticket. Practice builds skill, skill lowers fear, competence catches up as the feeling of confidence. Imposter feelings mean you are mid-bridge.
- **Parts:** Willingness to try; reps; skill; lower fear; competence; the feeling arrives last.
- **When to use:** The founder says they are not ready or not qualified for the next role, raise, or market.
- **How to score:** Feeling unqualified on a new goal is expected and neutral; refusing reps until it passes scores low.
- **Quote:** "you build the skills, which lowers the resistance and fear, which makes you competent, which then makes you catch up to feeling confident." (S003)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S007, S011, S016)

## 8. Calculated risk versus leap of faith *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Her line on quitting a job to go full time. A leap is acting on emotion; a calculated risk means you know cash flow, budget and the income you need, and cut spending to extend runway first. She reads aloud research that people who kept their day job at first were 33% less likely to fail (S003).
- **Parts:** Know the cash you need; cut costs to stretch runway; keep the day job while the business grows; then decide.
- **When to use:** Any founder about to quit, sign a lease, or pledge personal assets.
- **How to score:** Pass if runway and monthly need are written down; fail if the plan relies on motivation. Her own counterexample: she and her husband put the house up as collateral for his restaurants and ended up about $800K in debt (S001, S013).
- **Quote:** "too many people make the leap instead of making the decision." (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S003, S013). The marriage-on-a-first-date line in S003 is Adam Grant's, read by Mel.

## 9. Solo tour led by the future you *(the different tour)*
- **What it is:** Picture everyone whose opinion you keep checking as on a group tour. To decide, step onto a solo tour led by the future you. The problem is never your instincts but your fear of what others think.
- **Parts:** Leave the group; stop asking others what they think of your call; ask what the future you would choose; then run the body check (framework 10).
- **When to use:** A big call where cofounders, family or advisers all have opinions.
- **How to score:** Low if the reason is "everyone in my family does this" or "it's easier"; high if the founder can say why it is right for them.
- **Quote:** "You have to be your own tour guide in life." (S005)
- **Sources:** 2 (S005, S009)

## 10. Shrink or expand body check *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Get still, drop into the body, and ask whether the option opens possibility or shrinks you. Relief from avoiding something often hides a shrinking feeling; the right move feels expansive even when scary. Add a one-year fast-forward: can you picture yourself in this life a year from now?
- **Parts:** Get still (busyness makes doubt louder); picture the option; notice open versus shrink; fast-forward a year.
- **When to use:** Choosing between two paths, or deciding whether to make the call you keep putting off.
- **How to score:** Shrinks plus cannot picture it in a year means move; expands plus fear means the fear is pointing at the right thing.
- **Quote:** "Does it shrink you? Does it make you feel stuck? Do you lose your energy? That's a hell-frick-in-no." (S005)
- **Sources:** 2 (S005, S008)

## 11. Choose from pride, not guilt *(with the why-am-I-saying-yes test)*
- **What it is:** If the reason is "I should", "I have to" or "they will be mad", you are deciding from guilt. Switch to the action that makes you proud, or do not do it. Before any yes, ask whose reason it is.
- **Parts:** Catch the guilt words; ask why you are saying yes; if it is to avoid someone's disappointment, say no without apologizing; own the decision.
- **When to use:** Taking on a client, partner, or obligation; discounting for friends; staying in a role out of loyalty.
- **How to score:** Pass if the founder would make the choice with no one watching; fail if the main driver is not upsetting someone.
- **Quote:** "if you don't want to be controlled by guilt, just act in a way that makes you proud of yourself." (S004)
- **Sources:** 2 (S004, S009)

## 12. Quitter's Day and the missed-day reset
- **What it is:** Most people quit a new routine around day 19 (she cites Strava data; not verified here). Expect the dip and steer around it. A missed day is a bump, not falling off the wagon.
- **Parts:** Know the day; go back to your why; get encouragement from someone who believes in you; after a miss, say you just had a day and resume.
- **When to use:** Three weeks into a launch, sales push or new habit, when novelty is gone and results are not in.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a founder who quits in week three of a new channel before results could show has not tested it.
- **Quote:** "It's kind of like a pothole in the road, right? You just steer right around it if you see it." (S011)
- **Sources:** 1 (S011); the reset echoes S001 on what you do after you fail.

## 13. Boundaries, not balance
- **What it is:** Balance puts parts of life in competition and breeds resentment; boundaries are active choices from your top priority, communicated to others. Newer view: she chased balance and hundred-hour weeks for years, then dropped both (see dates below).
- **Parts:** Pick the top priority; set the boundary for yourself; tell people; protect breaks and sleep as business inputs.
- **When to use:** Founder burnout, a team in permanent emergency mode, a founder who equates busyness with success.
- **How to score:** Low if the founder describes 90 to 100 hour weeks as the plan; high if rest and boundaries are scheduled like work.
- **Quote:** "balance is something you wish for. Boundaries are what you need to create." (S016)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S014, S016). Dates: in 2022-12 she says she never left emergency mode and was addicted to being busy (S014); in 2023-12 she says self-care is a business strategy (S003) and also that she worked about 90 hours a week that year (S016). The newer stated view is boundaries; her practice lagged it.

## 14. Know your why
- **What it is:** When newness wears off, what keeps you going is knowing what you want and why. An idea without a reason does nothing.
- **Parts:** Write what you want; write why; reread it on the hard day.
- **When to use:** Motivation drops, or a founder has an idea but cannot say why it matters to them.
- **How to score:** High if the founder states the reason in one sentence without hedging.
- **Quote:** "When you know that, there is no fricking quitter's day for you because you know what you want and you know why you want it" (S011)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S011)

## 15. Pick one thing to master each year *(with the annual audit)*
- **What it is:** Choose one focus a year, become a student of it, then execute. She did this moving from speaking to social media, then podcasting, then YouTube. Before planning, audit the past 12 months.
- **Parts:** Audit last year; pick one thing; study people who did it; execute.
- **When to use:** A founder spread across too many channels or products.
- **How to score:** Pass if there is one named focus for the year; fail if everything is a priority.
- **Quote:** "And so pick something for the year that you want to master and become a student of it. Then execute." (S003)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S016)

## 16. Stop the train, open the door *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Reinvention starts by pausing and asking how you actually feel in your life and whether that is good enough. Then say the buried want out loud to someone you trust and consider it, with no obligation to act. Choose reinvention before a layoff or recession forces it.
- **Parts:** Stop; ask how you feel; say the want out loud; share it; consider it; take one small action.
- **When to use:** A founder on autopilot, or one who says they do not know what they want.
- **How to score:** She assumes people know what they want and have talked themselves out of it, so "I don't know" scores as discouragement, not missing information.
- **Quote:** "Think about saying the phrase out loud. And that opens the door to possibility." (S010)
- **Sources:** 1 (S010, a guest episode; these are Mel's own lines)

## 17. The 27 and the one *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Treat rejection as a count. You only need one yes, so keep going through the nos. Drawn from guest Hoda Kotb's story of 27 nos before one yes; the extraction is Mel's.
- **When to use:** Fundraising, sales, job hunting, pitching.
- **How to score:** A founder who stops after a handful of nos has not run the count.
- **Quote:** "as long as you don't quit, you can't lose." (S010)
- **Sources:** 2 (S010, S011)

## 18. Five zones of time
- **What it is:** Zone 1 is waking time, the only time that is yours; zone 2 starts when you check your phone or email; zone 3 is the workday traded for money; zone 4 is the afternoon fog; zone 5 is the end of the night, better spent on sleep. Her morning routine (the hot 15) protects zone 1.
- **When to use:** A founder who says they have no time for the strategic work.
- **How to score:** High if the most important work happens before the inbox opens.
- **Quote:** "And zone one, in my opinion, is the only time that you have that is yours. And you have to fight for it." (S003)
- **Sources:** 1 (S003)

## 19. Iceberg model and input becomes output
- **What it is:** The story you tell yourself becomes beliefs, then actions, then results, so fix it at the story level. Guard what goes in (news, feeds) because it shapes what comes out.
- **When to use:** The founder's actions keep contradicting their stated goals.
- **Quote:** "whatever it is that you allow as input profoundly impacts your output." (S003)
- **Sources:** 1 (S003)

## 20. Act on the action, not the outcome *(Dr. K's, adopted by Mel)*
- **What it is:** Put energy on what you can do now, not the result, because the payoff is not visible today. Mel adds that willpower is conflict you stay in, and that she names the dread before doing the hard thing.
- **When to use:** A founder paralyzed by outcomes they cannot control (investor decisions, market timing).
- **Quote:** "no, willpower is conflict and the ability to stay in it." (S002)
- **Sources:** 1 (S002)

## 21. Destination goal and minimally viable action *(Adam Alter's, adopted by Mel)*
- **What it is:** When globally stuck, set any goal out in the future to restart direction, then take the smallest step, run as an experiment rather than a pass or fail test. Mel applies it to her own podcast launch.
- **Quote:** "if you turn it into an experiment, this is no longer about getting it right, it's about getting yourself moving." (S006)
- **Sources:** 1 (S006)

## 22. Should to could *(Dr. Shadee Zarai's, adopted by Mel)*
- **What it is:** Catch "I should", reframe to "I could", list options, then commit to one to three "I will" steps. Part of Zarai's four A's of confidence (acceptance, agency, autonomy, adaptability); Mel's point is that each kind of self-doubt needs a different tool.
- **Quote:** "reframe it to I could, and then identify something that you will do." (S007)
- **Sources:** 1 (S007). Also guest-made and only restated by Mel: Carla Harris's could, should, want buckets for career choice (S015) and Tiffany Aliche's 70/30 living-cost target (S013).
