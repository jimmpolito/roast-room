# Stories: Mel Robbins

18 stories and cases she reuses, built from the four notes batches over S001 to S016. Each has the lesson she draws and source ids. Her own stories come first, most-used first; guest stories she retells are marked and come last.

1. **The $800,000 debt hole (2008).** She and her husband Chris cashed out savings, a home equity line and credit cards, and put up the house as collateral to expand his restaurants. The 2008 crash hit, she lost her job, liens and bankruptcy notices arrived, and at 41 with three kids under 10 she was drinking and screaming at Chris. She got out by holding several jobs at once and paying down cards. Lesson: "Like that literally is the answer that I realized nobody was coming." (S013) Waiting for motivation would have cost her the marriage and the house (S001, S011, S013, S014).

2. **The rocket-launch morning and the 5 Second Rule.** At the bottom, she counted down like a NASA launch to get out of bed, then used the same count to call friends and get out the door. It took weeks to work and she kept it secret for about three years. Lesson: act before you feel like it; "it doesn't work overnight. It works over time." (S001) She ties her later success back to this crisis: "It took me 10 years to realize that all of my success was born in a moment of crisis." (S014) (S001, S011, S014).

3. **The podcast she sat on for seven years.** She thought about it for years, made excuses (too late, too many podcasts), then put it on a friction list and did about 15 minutes a day for a year before launching at 56. Lesson: small daily moves unstick you long before the result, and excuses nearly cost her the thing she wanted most. "This podcast is something that I thought about for probably seven years, and it haunted me." (S006) (S002, S006, S014).

4. **The pivot from speaking to the podcast.** She spent years as a top corporate speaker, moved into social media, then the podcast, now YouTube. Before stopping the speaking she studied others for two years, because the fear of where the money would come from was real. Lesson: pick one thing, study it, then execute; leapfrog, do not leap blind. (S003) Her canceled daytime talk show led to the podcast, her favorite work: failures prepare you for something (S011). (S003, S011).

5. **Hundred-hour weeks and the 90-hour podcast year.** From 2014 she worked hundred-hour weeks and called it success, until nervous-system research in the pandemic stopped her (S003). In the podcast's hyper-growth year, after committing to heal, she still worked about 90 hours a week: "I worked way too much this year." (S016) She traces it to never leaving debt-era emergency mode (S014). Lesson: success built in crisis can leave you running your company like an emergency (S003, S014, S016).

6. **The hardest year and the friction list.** Burnout, two years living apart from her husband and son, and a close person stealing from and lying to her, while drinking to cope. A friend, Pete, told her she was the most miserable he had heard her and had her split a page into what she hates and what she loves. Lesson: friction is a signal to act, and every item becomes a project (S014). In S006 she tells a 2019 version with mentor Peter Sheehan, when life looked great and she still felt stuck (S006, S014).

7. **The partner who went bad.** "I had a partner who was fantastic and then that partner was really horrible." (S003) Lesson: do not bring in a partner to solve problems you have not diagnosed; hire the expertise first (S003).

8. **The team that outgrew its systems.** The podcast hit number five most followed on Apple Podcasts for the year, and her small team outgrew every process used to launch it, so they built new systems and studios. Lesson: frustration is a sign of growth; fix the system, not yourself (S016). Her team had mostly never done these jobs before: proof you can figure it out (S003).

9. **Credit card leapfrog and hidden debt.** Free cards at college registration led to $10,000 of debt, which she shuffled between cards. Low public defender pay pushed living costs onto cards, and she entered her marriage with five or six maxed cards she had not told her husband about. Lesson: easy credit plus shame becomes a cycle; hiding debt adds stress (S013).

10. **TEDx and the self-published book.** She took a free trip to give a TEDx talk in San Francisco with no speaking experience, gave it in a panic, ended with the five-second rule, and it went viral. She then self-published the book despite dyslexia and ADHD. Lesson: "too old" and "can't self-publish" are excuses you can walk through (S001).

11. **Law school and the wasted years.** She knew she did not want to be a lawyer and argued herself out of leaving. Lesson: "You will waste years, decades, even, of your fucking life moving in the wrong direction, because you will not slow down and tune into what you know is true." (S005) She does not regret it because it was her decision (S005).

12. **Dartmouth and the doubting teacher (1985).** A math teacher told her not to get her hopes up; she applied early decision anyway. "Oh, yeah, fuck off. Watch me." (S005) Lesson: one doubting voice can make you quit or fuel you (S005).

13. **Assuming her work would be noticed.** Early in her career she assumed others would see her contributions. Lesson: make your value known; "I think I made the mistake in my career early of just assuming that somebody else would notice that." (S015)

14. **Her dad's 80th birthday.** Her parents said not to come for a short weekend; she booked anyway, for herself and not from guilt, then stayed ten days and canceled work when his back surgery came up. Lesson: decide fast when a priority is clear, and choose from pride, not guilt (S004).

15. **Touring colleges with Oakley.** She became overbearing at her alma mater until Chris pulled her back; when her son asked her view, she told him only his mattered. He chose by whether his body felt open or closed. Lesson: other people's opinions, including a parent's, get in the way of your own call (S005).

16. **Selling the house where she raised her kids.** After 26 years she sold the Boston home; it was grief and a breakdown, but worth it. Lesson: hard changes can still be right, and you will wish you had started sooner (S011, S014).

17. **The unicycle rider (her retelling).** She saw a man on a unicycle on a New England highway and did not understand it. Lesson: "The only person the unicycle needed to make sense to was the guy riding it." (S009)

18. **Guest stories she retells (the lesson is hers, the story is not).** Hoda Kotb got 27 nos before one news director said yes: "as long as you don't quit, you can't lose." (S010) Wallow spent about 25 years in prison and built a media business with $1,000 and a plan: "And if Wallow can do this from a prison cell, you can do this from wherever you are right now." (S012) Jamie Kern Lima's 22 bank nos and QVC sellout: trust the present and let the dots connect (S008).
